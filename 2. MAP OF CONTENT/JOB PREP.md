# Job Prep

> Converting technical knowledge into job-readiness — resume, portfolio, interview preparation, and project ideas.

## Overview

Everything in the Skills Hub and Beyond Core builds capability. This section is about presenting that capability convincingly — turning skills into a resume that gets noticed, a portfolio that proves competence, and interview preparation that holds up under real questioning.

## What This Section Covers

- **Resume** — structuring and writing a resume that leads with impact, not just duties
- **Portfolio** — building a public body of work that demonstrates real analytical judgment
- **Interview preparation** — both technical questions (SQL, Excel, Power BI, Python, Statistics) and business/behavioral questions
- **Projects** — a set of scoped, varied project ideas to build toward a strong portfolio
- **Communication** — implicitly covered throughout, since every resume bullet, portfolio write-up, and interview answer depends on communicating clearly, not just knowing the material

## How These Pieces Fit Together

A resume gets you noticed → a portfolio proves what the resume claims → interview preparation demonstrates you can defend and reason about that work under real scrutiny. Weakness in any one of the three undermines the others — a strong portfolio with a weak resume never gets seen; a strong resume with no portfolio to back it up raises doubt; strong technical skills without interview practice can still fail to come across clearly in the room.

## Recommended Order

1. Build 3–5 portfolio projects first (see [[TOP 20 PROJECTS]] for ideas and [[PORTFOLIO BUILDING GUIDE]] for how to structure them)
2. Write the resume around those projects and any relevant experience (see [[RESUME TIPS]])
3. Prepare for interviews using both the technical notes throughout this vault and [[TOP 100 QUESTIONS]]
